# Governance and Succession Specification

## Governance domains

The protocol recognizes distinct authority domains:

- family governance;
- enterprise governance;
- treasury governance;
- protocol governance; and
- personal authority.

Authority in one domain does not automatically confer authority in another.

## Decision-specific governance

There is no universal voting formula. A governance policy may use:

- one eligible member / one vote;
- supermajority thresholds;
- ownership-weighted voting;
- role-based authority;
- multisignature approval;
- stewardship-qualified participation; or
- another explicit policy.

The policy must define eligibility, threshold, scope, duration, and amendment process.

## Family governance scope

Family governance may cover shared infrastructure, membership rules, archive policy, shared treasury policy, stewardship standards, collective initiatives, network administrators, and succession processes.

It must not automatically control independently owned enterprises or personal property.

## Founder and elder authority

Founders and elders may hold special scoped authority for genesis administration, constitutional guardianship, emergency response, or initial steward appointment.

Such authority must be constrained by scope limitations, multisignature requirements where appropriate, timelocks, succession rules, oversight, and termination conditions.

## Succession

Every critical role SHOULD define a succession policy containing:

- role identifier;
- current controller;
- successor or successor set;
- triggering conditions;
- recovery threshold;
- waiting period;
- required attestations; and
- effective authority after transition.

Potential triggers include death, incapacity, voluntary transfer, abandonment, removal, and cryptographic key loss.

## Recovery

Critical network functions should not depend on a single private key or administrator. Social recovery, multisignature, delegated recovery guardians, or another auditable policy should be used according to risk.

## Historical integrity

Changes in authority are events. They must not rewrite or conceal who previously held authority or why the transition occurred.
